Welcome to day 28: teach what you know.

## The Power of Teaching to Learn

Years ago in a train-the-trainer seminar, I learned something called *partner share*. You share what you just learned with somebody else, and the better you can articulate it, the better you know it. When you teach it, you're not just learning it again—you're integrating it with the knowledge that you already have. This reinforces the lesson in you.

Human beings are a mimicking species. You've heard the phrase "monkey see, monkey do"—we mimic. There is no greater teacher than your *diamond example*. But when you teach what you learn to the right people, the effect multiplies.

## Choose Your Audience Wisely

The key is *who* you share it with. Not everyone wants to be taught by you. I've learned this the hard way. Some people just want to be your friend and not your student. Some people see themselves as above you and they won't receive it anyway, and that's okay.

But when you find someone who does want to learn from you and you can share what you've learned in a way that they can receive it, it does two things:

**One:** It wires the information deeper into you. This is a huge benefit.

**Two:** It has an exponential effect on the people around you.

## Exponential Impact

We talked earlier about the power of exponential growth. If you start with a penny and double it every day for 31 days, you end up with almost $11 million. That's the power of small things multiplied.

Teaching what you know works the same way. When you share how a tool helped you, how a reframe shifted you, how breathing or art changed you in the moment, you're planting seeds in other people's lives that can keep growing long after the conversation ends.

## Teach Through Story

And remember, people's favorite story is their own story. When you teach, don't just dump information—tie it to a story. There's a saying in sales: *facts tell and stories sell*. Stories put people in a brainwave state where they can really listen, almost like a light trance.

So you might say something like: "Here's what was going on in my life then, and here's what I tried. Here's what's different now, and here's what I learned. Maybe you can use it too."

That's teaching from your diamond experience, not preaching from a pedestal.

## Your Mission for Day 28

As today, look for one person you can help. Someone at work, it could be a friend, a child, or a partner. Share one lesson you've learned in the Diamond Sprint. Give a story about how it helped you. Keep it simple: what was happening, what you did differently, and what changed. Then let it go.

You've just taught what you know, and in doing so, you locked it in more deeply for yourself.
